About

Thirty-five years on the floor. Now building what the floor needed.

RealAutomation is run by Mike Robinson — general manager, trainer, and the person who used to write the rota that never quite worked.

I've spent my working life in hospitality. Gastropubs, fine dining, high-volume restaurants and luxury hotels, in London and in Bermuda. Running floors, building teams, writing rotas, training people, and doing the admin that quietly consumes an operator's week.

Somewhere along the way I got tired of the gap. The software sold to independent businesses was either built for a 200-site group and priced accordingly, or it was a generic tool that had clearly never met a split shift, an allergen matrix or a Saturday double. So I started building what I actually needed — first for the business I was running, then for people who asked where I'd got it.

I work independently now — contracting with operators rather than running a single site. That's deliberate. It means I'm not fitting your project around someone else's service, and it means the person who scopes the work is the person who builds it and the person who trains your team.

That's the whole company. No sales team, no discovery framework, no account manager you've never met. An operator who learned to build, working with businesses the size of the ones I used to run.

What that changes

You don't have to explain your business to me. I know what happens when two people call off at four o'clock, why a new starter needs to be useful by Friday, and what an environmental health visit feels like when the paperwork is in a drawer. That shortens the discovery conversation by about two weeks, and it means the thing I build has the right defaults the first time you open it.

It also means I'll tell you when an app isn't the answer. Sometimes the fix is a process change, a better use of something you already pay for, or just a different habit. If that's the case, you'll hear it on the first call — I'd rather have a short honest conversation than a long expensive one.

Training is half the job

I'm a City & Guilds qualified trainer, and I've built training programmes and complete staff systems for restaurant groups and retail. Most software fails in hospitality not because it's bad but because nobody taught the team properly and it quietly stopped being used by week three. Training is part of every build here, not a line item you can decline.

London and Bermuda

I'm based in London with roots in Bermuda, and I work across both. Bermuda in particular has a lot of excellent independent businesses running on tools that don't fit them, and helping that scene — and the people coming up in it — is a long-term goal rather than a market opportunity.
